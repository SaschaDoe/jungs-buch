# Recommended Additions to the Library

Books and sources that would bring genuinely new empirical data or perspectives not covered by the current 16 books.

---

## Tier 1: Would Force Re-Evaluation of Existing Convergence Nodes

### 1. Daphna Joel — *Gender Mosaic: Beyond the Myth of the Male and Female Brain* (2019)

- **Based on:** Landmark 2015 PNAS study analyzing 1,400+ structural brain scans
- **Core finding:** Individual brains are unique mosaics of features — some more common in women, some in men — NOT distinct "male" or "female" brain types
- **What it challenges:** The biology cluster (Sax, Geary, Benenson) and even Eliot's moderate sex-differences framing. Forces the question: do convergence nodes like "biological aggression" and "differential emotional mirroring" describe real sex-typed brain architecture, or statistical artifacts at the group level that dissolve at the individual level?
- **Why it matters:** The neuroscience counter-position the library currently lacks entirely. Without it, the biology side of the convergence analysis presents a false consensus.
- **Publisher:** Little, Brown Spark

### 2. Tommy J. Curry — *The Man-Not: Race, Class, Genre, and the Dilemmas of Black Manhood* (2017)

- **Based on:** Social dominance theory data, psychology studies, sociological research on Black men and boys
- **Core finding:** Black males are disadvantaged *because of* their maleness, not despite it. "Patriarchy" as a universal explanatory framework breaks down when applied to Black men's actual social position.
- **What it challenges:** Connell's hegemonic masculinity framework (in the library) directly. Also hooks' feminist framing of Black masculinity. All 13 convergence nodes are implicitly about white/WEIRD boys — Curry's data forces testing whether they hold across racial positions.
- **Why it matters:** The library has zero books centering race-specific masculinity data with empirical grounding. Winner of the 2018 American Book Award.
- **Publisher:** Temple University Press

### 3. Cordelia Fine — *Testosterone Rex: Myths of Sex, Science, and Society* (2017)

- **Based on:** Meta-analytic evidence across evolutionary biology, endocrinology, neuroscience
- **Core finding:** The behavioral effects of testosterone are far more context-dependent and weaker than commonly claimed. Sex does not create fixed male/female natures — hormones, culture, and evolution interact in ways that make past gender dynamics "a serving suggestion, not a recipe."
- **What it challenges:** The "testosterone drives male behavior" framework shared by Sax, Geary, and Benenson. The biology cluster currently has no serious counter-voice — Fine IS that voice.
- **Why it matters:** Without Fine, the convergence analysis risks presenting a false consensus on biology. The actual scientific debate is far more contested. Winner of the Royal Society Science Book Prize.
- **Publisher:** W.W. Norton

## Tier 2: Fills Major Blind Spots

### 4. Peggy Orenstein — *Boys & Sex: Young Men on Hookups, Love, Porn, Consent, and Navigating the New Masculinity* (2020)

- **Based on:** Two years of in-depth interviews with 100+ boys and young men ages 16-22
- **Core finding:** Boys who hold egalitarian views in the abstract still default to 1955-era masculine scripts when describing the "ideal guy." Pornography has become the de facto sex education. Humor functions as an emotional suppression mechanism (empirical refinement of Pollack's "mask" concept).
- **Gap it fills:** None of the 16 books systematically addresses boys' sexual development with real interview data from the post-PornHub generation. The convergence nodes on "emotional expressiveness" and "mask/boy-code" are incomplete without understanding how these dynamics play out in the sexual domain.
- **Publisher:** Harper

### 5. Jonathan Haidt — *The Anxious Generation: How the Great Rewiring of Childhood Is Causing an Epidemic of Mental Illness* (2024)

- **Based on:** Collaborative review of published studies on smartphone/social media effects on adolescent mental health; dedicated chapter on boys
- **Core finding:** Unlike girls (harmed through social comparison and cyberbullying), boys are harmed through *displacement* of real-world risk-taking, physical play, and skill-building. Overprotection + digital substitution is especially damaging for boys' developmental trajectory.
- **Gap it fills:** The library has zero coverage of digital/algorithmic masculinity formation — arguably the biggest environmental change in boys' development since the oldest sources were published.
- **Supplement with:** Common Sense Media, *Boys in the Digital Wild* (2025). 73% of boys regularly encounter masculinity-related content online; 68% say it appeared algorithmically without searching for it. High-exposure boys have significantly lower self-esteem and higher loneliness. Academic research on the "Andrew Tate effect" (Haslop et al. 2024, Ringrose et al. 2025): 80% of British boys 16-17 have consumed Tate content.
- **Publisher:** Penguin Press

### 6. Louise Liddon & John Barry — *Perspectives in Male Psychology: An Introduction* (2021)

- **Based on:** First systematic BPS (British Psychological Society) academic textbook on male psychology as a distinct field
- **Core finding:** Standard depression instruments are sex-biased — they systematically under-detect depression in males because the symptom profile differs (irritability, substance use, risk-taking vs. sadness/withdrawal). Brings the measurement science underlying the clinical observation in Pollack/Kindlon/Thompson/Suefke.
- **Gap it fills:** (a) Adds psychometric evidence to the "hidden depression" convergence node; (b) Represents the positive masculinity / strengths-based perspective entirely absent from the library, which currently skews toward deficit/harm framing.
- **Publisher:** Wiley

## Tier 3: Adds Global/Cross-Cultural Data

### 7. Gary Barker / Equimundo — *The Man Box* study (2017) + *State of the World's Fathers* reports (2015, 2017, 2019, 2021, 2023)

- **Based on:** The Man Box Scale: first psychometrically validated cross-national masculinity norms instrument. Original study: 3,673 men ages 18-30 across US, UK, Mexico (later Australia, 30+ countries). State of the World's Fathers: only systematic global fatherhood dataset.
- **Core finding:** Men scoring high on the Man Box Scale are up to 5x more likely to bully, harass, or be violent, and ~2x more likely to experience depression or suicidal ideation.
- **Gap it fills:** Convergence nodes on "father hunger," "mask/boy-code," and "hidden depression" are all supported by clinical observation and Western samples. Man Box data provides quantitative cross-national validation (or complication). State of the World's Fathers grounds the "father hunger" node in global epidemiological data.
- **Reports freely available at:** equimundo.org

### 8. World Bank — *Educational Underachievement Among Boys and Men* (Welmond & Gregory, 2022)

- **Based on:** Most comprehensive global dataset on boys' educational underachievement — enrollment, retention, completion, and learning outcomes across income levels and regions worldwide
- **Core finding:** Almost 75% of middle-income countries and 95% of higher-income countries have fewer men than women enrolled at tertiary level. Includes what interventions have actually been tried and with what evidence for effectiveness.
- **Gap it fills:** The "schools-fail-boys" convergence node is currently grounded in Western (mostly US/UK/German) evidence. This report globalizes the node with dozens of countries across income levels. Also provides intervention-evaluation data — what has been tried and what worked.
- **Freely available at:** documents.worldbank.org

## Major Debates the Library Does Not Yet Represent

1. **The Gender Similarities Hypothesis (Hyde 2005, 2014):** 78% of psychological sex differences are small or trivial. The biology wing (Sax, Geary, Benenson) emphasizes differences; Fine and Joel represent the counter-position. This is THE central methodological debate in the field.

2. **The APA Guidelines Controversy (2018):** The APA's first-ever guidelines for psychological practice with boys and men declared "traditional masculinity is psychologically harmful." Triggered a major scientific debate (Ferguson 2022 vs. rebuttals 2023) about ideology vs. clinical science. None of the 16 books engages with this.

3. **Positive Masculinity / Strengths-Based Approaches:** The library is overwhelmingly deficit-focused. Only ~15% of articles in *Psychology of Men and Masculinities* have a positive focus. The positive masculinity framework (Kiselica, Englar-Carlson, Wilson et al. 2024) is absent.

4. **The Manosphere as Socialization Agent:** Academic research on the manosphere and its effects on boys (Haslop et al. 2024, Ging 2025, Ringrose et al. 2025) represents a genuinely new phenomenon postdating most of the library.
